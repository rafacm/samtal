# Store domain entities as validated JSON bodies and squash the chain: implementation

Companion to [`2026-08-23-json-body-store.md`](2026-08-23-json-body-store.md).
One section per milestone, recording what was actually written, the
deviations from the plan, what building it turned up, and the
verification results as they came out. A milestone with no deviations
says so explicitly.

## M1: reshape, squash, and re-floor in one atomic change

### What was done

Six commits: the schema and the squashed baseline with the
autogenerate entry point that produced it, the generic mapper pair,
the stranded-database refusal, the column-plant rewrites across the
suites, the body-parse family and its fixtures, and the record (ADR
addendum, workflow block, changelog, this document).

**The schema.** `db/schema.py` is five reshaped tables and two
untouched ones, exactly as decision 1 says. `providers` is
`stage`, `name`, `body`, `secrets`; `mcp_servers` is `name`, `body`,
`secrets`; `prompt_fragments`, `agents` and `agent_defaults` are their
key column and `body`, with the singleton check constraint kept on
`agent_defaults`. `devices` and `domain_settings` are byte-identical to
what they were. The body is `Text`, so `model_validate_json` is handed
what it wants and no encoder writes a quoted literal into a `JSON`
column. The docstring is rewritten around the new shape and says the
thing a reader would otherwise have to be told twice: no non-key model
field has a column, the first field that needs SQL-side filtering earns
one back through autogenerate, and the body keeps holding it either way.
`Boolean`, `Float` and `false` left the imports with the columns that
used them.

**The autogenerate entry point.** `db/migrations/autogen.py`, run as
`python -m vinga_server.db.migrations.autogen "<message>"`. It opens a
scratch database in a temporary directory, brings it to head through
`upgrade_to_head`, hands the connection to Alembic on
`config.attributes` exactly as `upgrade_to_head` does, and calls
`command.revision(..., autogenerate=True)`. `env.py` is untouched and
keeps its runtime refusal: the entry point satisfies it rather than
bypassing it, which is why there is still one place the database path
is decided. `migrations/` has no `__init__.py` and does not need one,
the implicit namespace package being enough for `-m`, and the wheel
carries the module (verified in the built artifact's listing).

**The baseline.** The four old revisions are deleted. The new one was
produced by the entry point above against an empty scratch database and
then reviewed as code: the generated `create_table` calls are
reproduced as they were written, and what changed in review is the
revision identity (`2001_json_body_baseline`, a fresh numbering block no
database was ever stamped with), the docstring, and the quoting. The
docstring records why the identity is fresh, that pre-reshape databases
are not upgradeable, and that the file was autogenerated and then read.

**The mapper pair.** `_from_row` validates `row.body` through
`descriptor.model` at the location the row's key columns address;
`_to_row` answers `{"body": entry.model_dump_json(exclude_unset=True)}`.
`_provider_from_row`, `_mcp_from_row`, `_agent_from_row`,
`_defaults_from_row`, `_layer_data`, `_provider_values`, `_mcp_values`,
`_layer_values` and `_agent_values` are gone, and with them the
`from_row`/`to_row` fields of `_Storage`, which is now the two write
checks and nothing else. The store's public interface is unchanged in
every signature; `OptionsReader` and everything above the store were
not touched at all. `_to_row`'s docstring carries the reasoning
decision 2 asks for in the place a reader meets it: why `exclude_unset`
is load-bearing rather than stylistic, why `exclude_none` is not used,
that the order is pydantic's declaration order, and the retroactive
consequence of a changed default.

**The read refusal.** `_body` is the read half of decision 3: a
`StorageError` naming the table, the identity and pydantic's field
locations, built inside the handler and raised outside it because a
`ValidationError`'s `errors()` carry the rejected input, which for a
body is the whole entity. It goes through `_validation_problems`, which
already holds every location to `safe_location`, so a key an operator
invented is never named either.

**The stranded-database refusal.** `migration_failure` grows one arm
ahead of the driver-message one, answering with the sentence that says
the database is stamped at a revision this build does not carry, that a
domain database written before the storage reshape is what that looks
like, and to delete `vinga.db` with its `-wal` and `-shm` companions and
re-seed, keeping any `conversations.db` beside it, naming the ADR. The
exception carries no `orig`, which is what the delta review's note is
about: the existing sentence would have reported the class name and
nothing else. The stored revision is not quoted back and neither is
Alembic's own wording.

The arm was written to match `CommandError` by type alone and to be
supplied by this module, and the PR review round below narrowed both:
it now takes four conditions rather than one (findings 1 and 2), and the
remedy names the file rather than the directory (finding 3). What is
described here is the first pass; the round records what it became and
why.

**The suites.** The column-planting sites are rewritten under the
plan's one rule. A plant of lawful state goes through a new
`tests/support/stores.body(entry)`, which sits beside `planted` with the
reason written down and, since finding 6 of the round below, produces
its body by calling `_to_row` rather than by restating what it does; a
plant of a malformed or old-shaped row is a hand-written body string,
because a dump is the one thing that cannot produce the shape the
reader is being asked to survive. `test_db_open.py`'s `EXPECTED_COLUMNS`
became the body columns per table and gained `HEAD`, and a fresh
database is now asserted to be at exactly that one revision with exactly
those columns. `test_provider_rows_hold_every_declared_model_field` is
replaced by the round trip in the body-parse family, with a comment in
its place naming the test that inherited its claim. The upgrade tests
(`_at_baseline`, `_seed_baseline_rows`,
`test_a_seeded_baseline_database_upgrades_to_head_with_every_value_kept`)
are deleted with the chain. `test_a_pre_upgrade_string_row_loads_and_is_written_back_unchanged`
moved to the body-parse family as
`test_an_mcp_list_written_as_plain_names_loads_and_is_written_back_unchanged`,
where its own fixture holds the old shape.

**The body-parse family.** `tests/unit/test_config_bodies.py` over
twelve committed bodies under `tests/unit/data/domain-bodies/`, one
directory per kind named as the registry names it, so the kind a body
belongs to is read off the tree rather than restated in a table (finding
5 below added the assertion that the tree still holds every kind the
registry declares, which is what stops the floor being lowered by a
deletion). Every
body validates through today's model; every body round trips through the
store (planted, loaded, dumped, planted, loaded) with the entry and its
`model_fields_set` unchanged; each kind has a body leaning on its
defaults and one writing its optional fields out; no body holds anything
under a secret-shaped key but an environment reference or a variable
name. The read-refusal sentinel plants a credential-shaped value inside
a body that will not validate and looks for it in the whole exception
chain, and a second one does the same for a body that is not JSON at
all.

**The workflow.** The wheel-migration step's added-columns block is
replaced by decision 7's two assertions: every entity table has a `body`
column, and the fresh database the wheel migrates is at exactly
`{"2001_json_body_baseline"}`. The comment above it says what it now
proves and names the two places in the suite where the shape is checked
in full (finding 7 below rewrote that comment, which had claimed a
parity with `EXPECTED_COLUMNS` that neither side enforced). The
expected-tables block and the whole conversations half are untouched.

### Deviations from the plan

**One, and it is a plan assumption that proved false.** Decision 3 says
the `_untransportable(..., numbers_only=True)` non-finite guard dies for
the four body kinds "because pydantic's JSON parser refuses NaN and
non-finite numbers at the door". It does not. Pydantic 2.13's JSON
parser reads the `NaN`, `Infinity` and `-Infinity` literals and produces
the corresponding floats. A declared float field with a constraint
catches its own (`tool_timeout_s` is `gt=0`, which NaN fails), but a
provider's options are passed through untyped and at any depth, so:

```
ProviderConfig.model_validate_json('{"type": "a", "nested": {"deep": [NaN]}}')
    .model_dump_json(exclude_unset=True)
    == '{"type":"a","nested":{"deep":[null]}}'
```

A stored non-finite number would therefore load happily and serialize as
`null` on every read and on the next write, which is precisely the
silently-different configuration the guard was written for and which
`test_a_stored_number_that_is_not_finite_cannot_be_read` and
`test_a_stored_number_that_is_not_finite_is_500` pin.

Per the working rule, the thread was stopped rather than redesigned: the
deletion the plan authorized on a false premise was declined, and the
guard survives for the body kinds in the least inventive form available,
asked of the validated entry inside `_body` instead of of a decoded
mapping inside `_stored`. Behaviour is what it was, including the
wording, on the read path.

One consequence on the write path is worth recording, because "behaviour
is what it was" is not quite true there: `_to_row`'s `model_dump_json`
serializes a non-finite float to `null` silently, where the old JSON
column stored the `NaN` literal for the read guard to catch on the way
back. `check_transportable` refuses such a fragment before it ever
reaches `_to_row`, so nothing a caller can write takes that path, which
makes this a loss of defense in depth rather than a change of behaviour.
It is named here so that a future change loosening `check_transportable`
knows what used to stand behind it.

The rest of decision 3's disposition holds as written: the
Mapping-shaped `_stored` path is off the body kinds entirely (it
survives for `_live_binding`, which assembles two JSON values into
`DomainConfig`), and `_mapping`, `_list` and `_shape_problem` survive
exactly where non-body values still need them, which is `devices`,
`domain_settings` and the two `secrets` columns.

**A reading, not a deviation.** Decision 6's fixture inventory lists "a
device" among the committed bodies. A device row is not a body: it has
no entity model and no descriptor, and decision 1 as amended by the plan
review's finding 5 leaves `devices` at its existing shape with its
hot-path column read. There is nothing for the body-parse family to
validate one through, so no device fixture exists; the device binding's
stored shape stays pinned where it already was, by
`normalize_device_bindings` on the load path and by
`test_a_json_column_of_the_wrong_shape_is_a_config_error[devices-agents-sam]`.

**A test that was written and then weakened, on the mutation's
evidence.** The body-parse family first asserted that a kind's bodies
between them write every field its model declares. Mutation 1 showed
that check turns an additive model change into a fixture edit, which is
the opposite of what the fixtures are for: these files exist to be old.
It is now the two-ends assertion described above, and its docstring says
why the stronger form was dropped.

### Discoveries

**Autogenerate's file naming.** `command.revision` with no `alembic.ini`
uses the default `file_template`, so the candidate arrived as
`03772ccb246d_json_body_baseline.py` with a hex revision id. Choosing
the identity is part of reviewing the candidate rather than something
the entry point does, and the review renamed both the file and the id.
A future non-baseline revision can keep the generated hex id; this one
could not, because the whole point of `2001_json_body_baseline` is that
no database carries it.

**Deleting every version file deletes the directory.** `git rm` of the
four revisions removed `versions/` itself, and Alembic will not run
without it. It is recreated by the baseline landing in it, so nothing in
the repository needs an empty directory, but a bisect between the two
commits would not have opened a database.

**`json_set` is the smallest way to corrupt one field of a body.** Two
suites reached into a row with raw SQL (`update agents set llm = ...`)
to plant a model-valid row that is not a valid deployment. They are
`update agents set body = json_set(body, '$.llm', 'ghost')` now, which
leaves the rest of the entry exactly as the writer wrote it.

### Deploying this

**The volume is reset together with the image bump, never after it.** A
build carrying this change cannot open a database written by the build
before it, and answers the attempt with the reset sentence rather than
starting, so the two are one step:

1. Stop the deployment.
2. Delete `vinga.db` from `server.database.dir`, together with its
   `-wal` and `-shm` companions. That file and no more: the
   conversations database lives in the same directory, is unaffected by
   this change, and must be kept, so "reset the directory" is the one
   thing not to do.
3. Roll out the new image and start it.
4. Re-seed the configuration from the files it is kept in, with
   `vinga-server config set ...` per entity, the same commands the
   example-fragment install suite runs on every CI run.
5. Restore the credentials with `vinga-server config set-secret`, which
   re-encrypts each one from the environment under the configured key.
   Nothing is restored from a backup of ciphertext, and nothing needs
   to be.

### Follow-ups

- **The infra re-seed is the operational half and lives in the infra
  repository, not this one.** The steps above are what it has to do; it
  runs on the day this image lands. Until it has run once, this change
  is proved in CI and not in production.
- **A ConfigMap change is inert without a rollout restart.** If the
  re-seed is driven from configuration held in the cluster rather than
  from files, remember that editing it does not restart anything on its
  own. An image bump usually hides this, and here the image bump and the
  configuration change arrive together, so it is worth checking rather
  than assuming.
- **The non-finite guard's home is worth one reviewer's opinion.** It
  survives inside `_body` because the plan's reason for deleting it was
  wrong, not because that is where it belongs. If pydantic ever gains a
  per-model `allow_inf_nan=False` that reaches pass-through extras, the
  check becomes a model setting and this line goes away.

### PR review round (PR #262)

External review of the milestone diff, 2026-08-23. Backend: claude CLI,
model `claude-opus-5`, read-only tool set (interim fallback tier). The
round is [posted on the PR](https://github.com/rafacm/vinga/pull/262#issuecomment-5384106902). Verdict as received: **mergeable after the
listed fixes**, with findings 1 to 3 named as the blockers ("the one
operator-facing sentence this milestone exists to produce currently
fires for the wrong failures, on the wrong database, and recommends
deleting data that the same PR's ADR says to keep") and 4 and 5 as "the
two gates the squash removed and the milestone claims to have
replaced". Findings condensed but faithful:

1. **P2: the `CommandError` arm answers every Alembic command failure
   with the reset sentence.** The docstring's universal is false: the
   same exception covers an unreadable script directory, a chain with
   two heads, and a database stamped by a NEWER build and then met by a
   rolled-back image, whose remedy is to roll forward rather than to
   destroy a current volume. Narrow it to the resolution failure, or
   compare the stored revision against the chain, and cover the
   rollback case.
   *Resolution* (`0eff30b4`): four conditions, not one. A
   `CommandError`, a cause chain holding Alembic's own
   `ResolutionError`, a caller that handed in a superseded set, and a
   revision inside that closed set of the four ids the squash deleted,
   which is the only thing that tells a stranded database from one
   written by a later chain. Everything else falls through to the
   sentence it took before the arm existed. The rollback case, an
   unreadable script directory and a bare `CommandError` are each
   pinned as not getting the reset sentence.

2. **P2: the sentence fires for the conversations database too**,
   through the shared `open_at`, with advice about the wrong file whose
   deletion would destroy recorded conversations the store erases
   physically only on request. The ADR addendum says the conversations
   chain is untouched and nothing here reaches it; the code reached it.
   *Resolution* (`249183d8`): what a stranded database is told became a
   parameter of `open_at`, `Superseded`, carrying one chain's deleted
   revisions and the sentence they earn. `open_database` supplies the
   domain's; `open_conversations` supplies none and says why. Both
   directions are pinned with `0001`, a live revision of one chain and a
   deleted one of the other, so what decides is which chain was opened.

3. **P2: "Reset the database directory" over-deletes** and contradicts
   this document's own step 2: both databases live under
   `server.database.dir`, and the changelog says in one bullet to reset
   the directory and to keep `conversations.db`.
   *Resolution* (`228d8d20`): the file is named in all four places (the
   refusal, the changelog bullet, the ADR addendum, the deploy step):
   delete `vinga.db` with its `-wal` and `-shm` companions, keep any
   `conversations.db` beside it. The baseline's docstring picks up the
   same wording.

4. **P2: nothing checks that the single baseline still matches
   `db.schema.metadata`.** With no successor revision, a column added to
   `schema.py` without a migration is invisible until a deployment
   write fails, and column types, nullability, primary keys and the
   singleton constraint were pinned nowhere.
   *Resolution* (`3681b33e`):
   `test_the_baseline_builds_exactly_what_the_tables_declare` opens a
   database at head and asserts `compare_metadata` against the metadata
   is empty, which is the comparison autogenerate itself makes. Proved
   by two mutations, both recorded in the table below.

5. **P2: the body floor derives its inventory from the tree**, so
   deleting a kind's directory or a transport's bodies leaves the whole
   family green while the ADR goes on promising them.
   *Resolution* (`f0d016ef`): the kinds are asserted equal to the entity
   registry's names and the transports equal to the set the transport
   field's own annotation declares. Both were proved by deleting: the
   provider directory, and the two stdio bodies, each fails this test
   and nothing else.

6. **P3: the round trip never calls `_to_row`**; its dump half was a
   copy of the `exclude_unset` decision in test support, so removing
   `exclude_unset` from the shipped writer left the family green while
   its docstring claimed to pin exactly that.
   *Resolution* (`b51522e3`): `stores.body` delegates to `_to_row`,
   finding the descriptor by the entry's model (exact match, since
   `AgentConfig` is an `AgentDefaults`). With the copy gone, removing
   `exclude_unset` fails eight round trips. The reach past the
   underscore is deliberate and the helper says so.

7. **P3: the workflow comment claims a parity with `EXPECTED_COLUMNS`
   that does not exist**: CI holds five table names and checks for a
   body column, the suite holds full column sets and checks equality.
   *Resolution* (`cb1046ac`): the comment says what the block is, a
   packaging check deliberately asking less, and names the two places
   the shape is really checked.

8. **P3: "one sparse and one fully written per kind" is untrue of
   `prompt-fragment`**, whose model has no optional field, and the test
   is vacuous for that kind.
   *Resolution* (`4772ab6b`): the ADR promises both ends where a kind
   has two ends, and the test's docstring names the single-body case and
   points at the inventory assertion, which is what holds that kind.

**On the flagged deviation, as received:** "The NaN guard's survival is
correct and should stand. ... the plan's premise ('pydantic's JSON
parser refuses NaN and non-finite numbers at the door') is indeed false
for the one kind that can hold an untyped number at arbitrary depth.
Keeping the check inside `_body`, asked of the validated entry,
preserves the existing refusal and its wording ... The declined deletion
was the right call; the follow-up note recording why it lives in `_body`
rather than in the model is the right disposition." The one consequence
it asked to be recorded, that the write side now serializes a
non-finite float to `null` where the old column stored the literal, is
in the deviation section above (`1941d8ec`).
